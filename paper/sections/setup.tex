\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We train and evaluate on SWE-rebench agent trajectories that were recorded with OpenHands
\citep{swerebench,swerebenchtraj,openhands}. Each trajectory is rendered as text in message order: the message text, each
tool call as JSON of its name and arguments, and each tool output verbatim. The system prompt,
which is the same in every trajectory, is omitted. We tokenize with the GPT-2 byte-pair vocabulary
and use the first 16,384 trajectories of the dataset. A hash of the repository name assigns each
repository to training or evaluation, and no repository therefore occurs in both: 15,680
trajectories (1.65B tokens) form the training set and 704 trajectories (69.8M tokens) are held
out. A training example is a window of 16,384 consecutive tokens from one trajectory. Trajectories
are drawn with probability proportional to their length, and the start of the window is uniform.

Every model is a stack of blocks, each of which applies a sequence mixer and then an MLP with one
hidden layer of twice the model width and GELU activations; both are preceded by RMS normalization
and have a residual connection. The mixer is either Gated DeltaNet (GDN) \citep{yang2025gdn}, in the
implementation of the flash-linear-attention library, or causal softmax attention with rotary
position embeddings. Both use heads of width 64 and a causal depthwise convolution of kernel size
4 on queries, keys and values. The input embedding and the output layer are separate matrices. $A$ counts the
softmax-attention layers of a model and $P$ the placements of \ingram{}. With 8 blocks, a 3:1
hybrid has attention in blocks 4 and 8 and a 7:1 hybrid in block 4; the deeper 3:1 hybrids have
attention in every fourth block. The dose grid of Section~\ref{sec:types} also has models with no
attention and with attention in every second block. We use three sizes: 8 blocks of width 256, 12 blocks of width 384
and 16 blocks of width 512. Unless we state otherwise, a model has 8 blocks of width 256, and
\ingram{} has the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:method-config}.

All models train with AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$), a weight decay of 0.1 on weight
matrices other than the embedding and the hashed table, and gradient clipping at norm 1. A step is
one window, and 10,000 steps therefore amount to 164M tokens. The learning rate rises linearly
over 200 steps and then follows a cosine to 1\% of its peak at the end of the run. For each model
of Table~\ref{tab:lr} we selected the peak among two or three values on seeds 0 and 1. The 7:1
hybrids and the remaining cells of the dose grid have no selection of their own and use
$4 \times 10^{-3}$ without \ingram{} and $2 \times 10^{-3}$ with it (Appendix~\ref{app:lr}). Training
uses bfloat16 autocast, with the reads and writes of \ingram{} in 32-bit floats, on one rented
RTX~4090 per run (Appendix~\ref{app:compute}).

We evaluate every model on the same 32 held-out windows of 16,384 tokens. \emph{Loss} is the mean
negative log-likelihood per token on these windows, in nats. We selected learning rates and cue
lengths on seeds 0 and 1 (Tables~\ref{tab:lr} and~\ref{tab:cues}). Confirmatory comparisons use
seeds 20--27, which were not used for selection: $n = 8$ runs per arm, paired by seed. A seed sets
the initialization and the order of the training windows: step $k$ of seed $s$ draws its window
from a generator seeded with $1000s + k$, two seeds that differ by one therefore train on the same
windows at an offset of 1,000 steps, and both models of a pair train on the same windows. For each
comparison we committed the arms, the metric and the test to the repository before the runs of its
new arms started; in rows 1 to 4 and 7 of Figure~\ref{fig:confirm}, one of the two arms consists
of runs from an earlier comparison. The
test is a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the paired differences, with a Holm correction
inside each pre-registered family of two to four tests. With $n = 8$ the smallest attainable $p$
is $1/256 = 0.0039$; the Holm-adjusted values $0.008$, $0.012$ and $0.016$ in our tables therefore
all indicate that the eight differences share one sign. We write $\Delta$ for the loss of the
model with \ingram{} minus the loss of the reference, and report its mean with a 95\%
percentile-bootstrap interval over seeds (10,000 resamples). Results on seeds 0 and 1 are
exploratory and are labelled as such. The code that produced these results, the configuration, log and
per-token losses of every run, a reference implementation of \ingram{} and the pre-registration
files with their commit times are available at \url{https://github.com/no-way-labs/in-gram}.
